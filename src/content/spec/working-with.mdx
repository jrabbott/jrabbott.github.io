### How to engage

- **Agree the destination first**: Outcomes, constraints, and success measures before method talks.
- **Hand over a domain**: Clear authority, then short progress reviews instead of constant check-ins.
- **Surface risk while it is cheap**: Facts and options early, not post-mortems after the window closes.
- **Leave the path open**: Once the goal is clear, trust independent judgement on how to get there.

### Communication that works

- **Lead with the point**: Then the evidence. Long warm-ups waste energy on both sides.
- **Tie feedback to outcomes**: Decisions land better when they reference results, not vibes.
- **Meetings need a job**: Agenda, active tasks, and a decision or next step.
- **Account for written load**: Dyslexia makes long, high-volume written threads harder than verbal clarification. AI helps with proofreading and structure; see [AI as an accessibility tool](/blog/posts/ai-accessibility-tool/).

### Where I add most value

- Complex public-sector and platform delivery where architecture, delivery, and ops have to stay aligned.
- Technical direction that stays hands-on: setting patterns, unblocking teams, and shipping.
- Platform, DevOps, and quality practices that make the next release safer, not just the current one.
- Mentoring and standards so the team still holds together after handover.

### Best fit / friction

**Best fit**

- Clear ownership and measurable targets.
- Autonomy on method after goals are agreed.
- Direct, fact-based collaboration with product and delivery.
- Work where urgency and risk identification matter.

**Friction**

- Vague goals with no agreed definition of done.
- Meeting-heavy process that avoids decisions.
- Low-autonomy execution with little ownership of the domain.
- Written-only cultures that leave no room to clarify in conversation.
