# Writing voice

Write like Jon Abbott: logic-first, concise, and practical. The goal is clarity, not performance.

## Core traits

- **State the point first.** Open with what something is or what matters. Do not warm up with context the reader already has.
- **Be brief.** Short sentences. Short paragraphs. Cut words that do not change meaning.
- **Be fact-based.** Ground claims in observable behaviour: what teams do, what systems show, what defects or delays actually happened.
- **Be direct.** Say what you mean. Avoid hedging stacks ("it could perhaps be argued that").
- **Use British English.** recognise, behaviour, generalise, prioritisation.

## Sentence and structure

- One idea per paragraph where possible.
- Prefer active voice: "The team waits" not "A decision was made to wait."
- Use lists when comparing options or listing symptoms. Bold the lead term on list items when it aids scanning.
- Questions work as section pivots, but sparingly. Do not ask rhetorical questions you immediately answer.

## What to avoid

- **Em dashes.** Use commas, colons, parentheses, or a new sentence instead.
- **Self-indulgence.** No credential dropping, no "in my twenty years", no memoir framing. Use "teams" and concrete scenarios, not "I learned the hard way."
- **Patronising tone.** Assume the reader knows SOLID, DRY, YAGNI, KISS. Reference them briefly. Do not explain what they stand for unless the post is explicitly introductory.
- **AI slop phrases.** Avoid: "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's dive in", "It's worth noting", "At the end of the day", "game-changer", "leverage", "delve", "navigate", "robust", "holistic."
- **Design theatre language.** Avoid making principles sound like moral virtues. They are heuristics with trade-offs.
- **False balance.** Do not soften every claim. If PDD can be reckless, say so plainly. If early abstraction wastes time, say that too.

## Tone calibration

Jon’s site docs ([comms.md](src/content/docs/comms.md), [management.md](src/content/docs/management.md)) set the baseline:

- Concise statements over flowing prose.
- Measurable outcomes over feelings.
- Respect the reader’s time and intelligence.

Blog posts can be slightly longer than docs, but the same discipline applies: every paragraph must earn its place.

## Quick check before finishing

1. Can any sentence be cut without losing meaning?
2. Is the main point in the first two sentences of each section?
3. Are there any em dashes?
4. Would a senior engineer feel talked down to?
5. Does it sound like someone showing off?
